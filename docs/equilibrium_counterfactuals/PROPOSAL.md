# When Can You Trust an Equilibrium Counterfactual?
## Certified interventional semantics for learning and equilibrium economies — a flagship proposal for causalrl 2.0

## The pitch

Every equilibrium model in economics answers policy questions by computing `do()` at a fixed point: impose the intervention, re-solve, read off the counterfactual. Every agent-based and learning model answers the same question by unrolling adaptive dynamics and looking at where they go. The entire equilibrium-vs-complexity fault line — Part I of EECS IV (Geanakoplos; Pangallo's "Equations vs. Maps"; Brock & Hommes), Dogra's "Paradoxes and Problems in the Causal Interpretation of Equilibrium Economics," and ultimately the Lucas critique itself — is a disagreement about **when those two `do()` operators return the same answer**. Nobody has made that question formal, computable, and certifiable. The causalrl 2.0 stack is, to my knowledge, the only toolchain that contains all four required pieces at once: cyclic SCMs with solution-function semantics (equilibrium `do()`), `build_unrolled_scm` (dynamics `do()`), causal Markov games with learning populations, and a typed certificate layer with an honest IDENTIFIED/BOUNDED/EMPIRICAL taxonomy.

**Central thesis (the one-sentence version):** *the interventional predictions of a population of learning agents are point-identified by the equilibrium model only under an expectational-stability condition, are partially identified by the coarse-correlated-equilibrium polytope under a no-regret condition, and are not identified at all in provably-nonconvergent regimes — and each of the three cases can be detected and certified automatically.*

This is simultaneously a causal-inference theory paper (interventional semantics of multi-agent learning systems, extending the ODE→SCM line), a learning-in-games paper (what CCE convergence buys you *causally*), and an economics paper (the Lucas critique as a machine-checkable certificate). That triple-audience structure is what makes it frontier rather than incremental.

## Three theory targets

**T1 — E-stability is causal validity (point identification).** For an economy/game whose equilibrium is a simply-solvable fixed point of a best-response/expectation map, and adaptive learning whose mean dynamics are the associated ODE, prove: equilibrium `do()` coincides with the learning-limit `do()` for local interventions **iff** the equilibrium is stable under the mean ODE — i.e., exactly the E-stability condition of Evans & Honkapohja (and the convergence conditions of Marcet–Sargent). This formalizes and unifies two literatures that have never been connected: the adaptive-learning macro literature's E-stability and the cyclic-SCM literature's equilibration/solvability conditions (Mooij–Janzing–Schölkopf's ODE→SCM construction; Bongers–Forré–Peters–Mooij solution-function semantics and σ-separation). Honest framing: T1 is a synthesis-and-formalization theorem — its value is the bridge, and the bridge is real: "E-stability" becomes "the σ-solution is the causally correct object," a statement neither literature can currently make.

**T2 — CCE partial identification (the bridge result; repositioned after due diligence).** For finite games populated by no-regret learners, time-averaged play converges to the coarse-correlated-equilibrium set (Hart–Mas-Colell/Foster–Vohra; folklore with finite-time rates). Therefore, for an intervention `do(x)` that modifies the game to `G_do`, the time-averaged outcome functional of the learning population lies in `[min, max]` of that functional **over CCE(G_do)** — a polytope, so both endpoints are linear programs. Every atomic piece of this exists somewhere: set-valued counterfactuals under payoff/action changes over BCE (Bergemann–Brooks–Morris 2022), LP-computable identified sets for counterfactual objects over equilibrium polytopes with an asymptotic-no-regret justification (Magnolfi–Roncoroni; Syrgkanis–Tamer–Ziani), no-regret as the identifying behavioral assumption (Nekipelov–Syrgkanis–Tardos 2015), set-valued `do()` quantified over equilibrium sets in a causal formalism via pre-policy interventions (Hammond et al. 2023; Mishra–Fox 2024), and LP-over-CCE machinery through to mean-field games (Campi et al. 2026). **The delta this paper claims** is therefore precisely: (i) the *finite-time, anytime-valid* certified version — the bound binds at the horizon actually run, with endpoints inflated by *measured realized regrets*, abstaining otherwise, rather than an asymptotic statement; (ii) the identification **trichotomy** — point under E-stability, set under no-regret, hedge under non-convergence — stated against the *cyclic-SCM equilibrium `do()`* as two semantics for one query, which no prior work compares; (iii) the bound *width* as the operational measure of how much predictive content "equilibrium" has for adaptive populations under that intervention. Feasibility caveat to state plainly: the LP lives in joint-profile space (exponential in players; hardness results exist for succinct games), so exact bounds are for small games, sampled/relaxed bounds beyond.

**T3 — Certified failure (non-identification with diagnostics).** In regimes where the mean dynamics at the equilibrium are unstable or admit attracting cycles/chaos — cobweb dynamics under naive expectations (Hommes; Brock–Hommes' rational route to randomness), and chaotic learning in simple games (Sato–Akiyama–Farmer, PNAS 2002 — a fitting testbed, given Farmer edits the volume that frames this whole agenda) — characterize the gap between equilibrium `do()` and realized interventional behavior, and pair it with runtime diagnostics (Jacobian spectrum at the σ-solution; Lyapunov-exponent estimates from `TrajectoryLog`s) that trigger `EMPIRICAL`-or-hedge certificates. T3 may land as characterization-plus-certified-diagnostics rather than a clean theorem; that is acceptable — the *certificate behavior* is the contribution.

## What the library does (and the two small additions needed)

The headline API is the Phase 5 comparator, promoted from utility to scientific instrument: `compare_equilibrium_unrolling(cyclic_scm, unrolled_factory, do, ...) -> Certificate`, returning `IDENTIFIED` when the T1 solvability+stability check passes (with the Jacobian spectrum as witness), `BOUNDED` with the LP-computed CCE interval when only no-regret is established (T2), and `EMPIRICAL`/hedged with the instability diagnostic otherwise (T3). Supporting cast, all already specced: cyclic `solve` + σ-separation; `build_unrolled_scm` for the dynamics side; `magames` typed populations for the learners; `TrajectoryLog` for the learning logs; conformal wrappers for finite-sample versions of the empirical checks.

Two additions the spec does not yet contain, both small and domain-agnostic: a `cce_polytope(game)` constructor plus LP bound routine in `magames` (finite games; the constraints are standard), and a `stability_margin` diagnostic on cyclic solutions (Jacobian spectral abscissa, reported in certificate provenance — the tipping-gamma of this problem). Both belong in core under the generality rule.

## Experimental program

**E1 — Cobweb, the canonical stage.** One model, three regimes (stable / unstable / chaotic via the slope ratio and expectation rule, following Hommes and Brock–Hommes). Intervention: a cost shock or tax `do()`. Show the full certificate ladder fire correctly: point-identified agreement in the stable regime, CCE bounds in intermediate cases, hedged divergence in the chaotic one — with the equilibrium prediction *quantitatively wrong* exactly where the certificate says not to trust it.

**E2 — Learning chaos as adversarial test.** The Sato–Akiyama–Farmer game: Nash `do()` point predictions miss; the T2 CCE interval contains the realized time-averaged interventional outcome. The narrative writes itself: the same complexity-economics lineage that produced the critique produces the counterexample the certificate catches.

**E3 — Do deep-RL populations respect the bounds?** Independent PPO/Q-learners (magames + the d3rlpy path) in Cournot/Bertrand pricing. Deep-RL agents are only approximately no-regret, so whether their interventional time-averages fall inside CCE(G_do) is a real empirical question — either answer is a finding (validation of the bound's practical reach, or a measured violation quantifying the no-regret approximation error).

**E4 — The macro loop where the paradox bites.** A small New-Keynesian-style or leverage-cycle toy expressed as a cyclic SCM (the Dogra setting), with a policy-rule intervention: exhibit a policy conclusion that *flips* between equilibrium `do()` and learning `do()` in the E-unstable region, certified. This is the economics-facing centerpiece.

**E5 (garnish, not gate) — Scale.** A JAX population run demonstrating certificate throughput on large learner populations; supports the library paper, not the theory.

## Positioning after due diligence (completed — see companion report)

The verdicts: **T1 survives** — targeted searches found no formal bridge between E-stability/adaptive learning and SCM equilibration; the theorem appears unclaimed. Two refinements it must absorb: state the clean version within the simple-solvability class, and handle the general case with CCM-style semantics, because Blom–Bongers–Mooij (UAI 2019) prove plain SCMs cannot represent equilibria that depend on initial conditions — which is exactly *equilibrium selection by learning basins*, so the caveat and the economics are the same phenomenon and the paper should say so. **T2 narrows** to the bridge-and-operationalization contribution described above; its first paragraph must credit the four adjacent literatures, and the paper's identity is explicitly the synthesis plus two bridges. **T3 unaffected**, and gains Dash (AISTATS 2005) — equilibration changes causal structure, so manipulating the equilibrated graph misleads — as its causal-side precursor.

Foundations to cite: Mooij–Janzing–Schölkopf and Rubenstein et al. (ODE/dynamic → SCM), Bongers–Forré–Peters–Mooij (cyclic SCMs, σ-separation), Blom–Bongers–Mooij (CCMs), Bongers–Blom–Mooij (SDCMs), Iwasaki–Simon and Dash (equilibration), **White & Chalak (JMLR 2009, settable systems — the PCM extension explicitly built for optimization, equilibrium, and learning; framework-level prior art, mandatory citation)**, Evans–Honkapohja, Marcet–Sargent, Evans–Ramey (learning and the Lucas critique), Hart–Mas-Colell and the AGT no-regret canon, Bergemann–Brooks–Morris, Magnolfi–Roncoroni, Syrgkanis–Tamer–Ziani, Nekipelov–Syrgkanis–Tardos, Hammond et al. and Mishra–Fox (causal games, pre-policy interventions), performative prediction (Perdomo et al.) as the single-learner cousin, and Dogra plus EECS IV Part I for the economics framing. Residual pre-submission checks (cheap): read Hammond et al.'s pre-policy and settable-systems sections in full; read Mishra–Fox completely; pin down Magnolfi–Roncoroni's published ANR treatment; scan EC 2024–2026 proceedings and recent Mooij-group preprints; verify the CE-optimization complexity citations exactly.

Main risks: T2 bounds may be wide in interesting games (mitigation: the width *is* a finding — it measures how little "equilibrium" constrains adaptive behavior under intervention, reported as such); T1's generality may narrow under scrutiny (mitigation: state it for the linear/contractive class where cyclic-SCM identification is solid, exactly the library's exact-claims class); the economics audience may say "we knew learning matters" (mitigation: they did not have point-vs-partial-vs-non identification as a theorem, LP-computable bounds, or a certificate that says which case you are in).

Venues and splitting: the natural cut is (1) the theory+certificates paper at **ACM EC or NeurIPS** (EC is the perfect learning-in-games/econ audience for T2), with the causal-semantics core optionally at **CLeaR**; and (2) the economics-facing paper — "The Lucas critique as a computable certificate" — at **JEDC**, carried by E1/E4. The library ships the comparator and CCE module either way, feeding the JMLR-MLOSS/JOSS software paper.
